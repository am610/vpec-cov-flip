VPEC_COV: main findings (last updated 2026-10-22)

Goal
  Put the correlated peculiar-velocity covariance from FLIP (arXiv:2501.16852)
  into the factorized form C = D + B B^T (D diagonal, B is N x K) used by SNANA
  PR #1797, so the likelihood is solved with the Woodbury identity instead of an
  N x N Cholesky.

Caveat
  All numbers come from a synthetic sample (uniform on the sky, 0.02 < z < 0.10)
  with the power spectrum bundled with FLIP. They show the method and are not a
  forecast. Accuracy was tested at N=1500 only. Timing was tested up to N=20000
  with random B (shapes only).

1. The FLIP velocity covariance is not low-rank
   Modes needed for a fraction of the variance (script 02):
        N     K90    K99   K99.9
       250     88    202     236
      1000    166    617     860
      4000    213   1385    2830
     10000    232   1831    5414
   K90 stays near 230. K99 and K99.9 keep growing, but slower than N
   (K99/N = 0.18 at N=10^4).
   Corentin's numbers (500 to 700 modes for 99% at N=2500, ZTF) are the same
   order as ours (1211 at N=3000).

2. How to build B
   eig    top-K eigenmodes. Needs a full eigendecomposition.
   rsvd   randomized range finder on C (about 4 products C @ [N x K]).
          Matches eig without the eigendecomposition.
   mc     K random draws x ~ N(0, C), B = [x_1 ... x_K] / sqrt(K).
          This is the random-realization scheme proposed by Rick Kessler.
   Remainder correction: add diag(C - B B^T) back into D. It removes most of the
   log|C| bias (about -12 at K=200 without it, about -0.2 with it).

3. Accuracy on fs8 (script 03, N=1500, 30 mock residuals)
   Shift of the fitted fs8 relative to the dense fit, in units of sigma_fs8
   (rms, with remainder correction):
        method     K=100   K=200   K=400
        eig         0.21    0.11    0.04
        rsvd        0.24    0.11    0.07
        mc          1.11    0.69    0.52
   eig and rsvd: no mean shift. mc: mean shift of -1.0, -0.6, -0.4 sigma at
   K=100, 200, 400. The sign and size are the same for different random seeds
   (8 seeds tested at each K), so the mc bias is systematic and not sampling
   noise.

4. Time per likelihood evaluation (script 04, 8 threads)
        N      K    dense      woodbury    cached
      2000    200    31 ms      2.7 ms      0.30 ms
      5000    200   482 ms      6.7 ms      0.38 ms
     10000    200   2.4 s       13 ms       0.35 ms    (183x and 6940x)
     10000    400   2.5 s       27 ms       1.1 ms     (92x and 2190x)
     20000    200   13 s        52 ms       0.27 ms    (261x and 49700x)
   woodbury: FactorizedCovariance rebuilt every call (needed if D changes, for
             example sigv free or an fs8-dependent remainder term).
   cached:   B^T D^-1 B computed once (valid when D is fixed and fs8 only
             rescales B).
   Below N of about 2000 a dense Cholesky takes a few ms, so there is no gain.
   Timings vary by tens of percent between runs. Read the speedups as orders of
   magnitude.

5. Consequences
   - Corentin's pipeline (N about 2500) will probably not gain from Woodbury.
   - The gain is for LSST-like N (several thousand and above).
   - For SNANA: use rsvd or eig plus the remainder correction. Random
     realizations need a larger K or a bias correction before use.
   - rsvd needs the dense covariance in memory (0.8 GB at N=10^4).

Open questions
   1. Accuracy at N above 5000, with the production power spectrum and the real
      sample geometry, and for w0, wa instead of fs8.
   2. Can the mc bias be corrected, or should SNANA use rsvd?
   3. How should D and the remainder term be handled when sigv is fitted?
   4. Impact on the w0, wa FoM for the M25 and K25 samples (archived outputs).

Status
   The forecast project (re-analysis of M25 and K25 with cov(vpec)) has not been
   started. Corentin asked that it wait for discussion in the PV team and
   DESC-TD. This directory is outside SNANA and is not shared yet.
